Grades and face quality

Face and back quality are stated separately. The commercial offer should always identify both the named grade system and the defect limits or approved reference sample.

European / EN appearance classes

EN 635 uses appearance classes E, I, II, III and IV. Common commercial combinations include E/I, I/II, II/II, II/III, III/III, III/IV and IV/IV, subject to mill programme.

Birch plywood export grades

Common Russian and international birch-plywood notation includes B (I), BB (II), CP (III) and C (IV), supplied in combinations such as B/BB, BB/BB, BB/CP, CP/CP, CP/C and C/C.

Chinese commercial grade notation

Common purchasing descriptions include A/A, A/B, B/B, B/BB, BB/CC, C/D and D/E. These names are market conventions and can vary between suppliers, so visual samples and written defect tolerances are part of the purchase specification.

Film-faced and marine options

For marine interiors, boatbuilding components and other wet-service applications, we quote against the buyer’s named standard and service conditions. A marine-use specification normally addresses durable exterior bonding, veneer quality, core gaps, panel construction, tolerances, formaldehyde class and documentary evidence.

Important. “Marine” is not treated as a stand-alone grade name. Compliance with BS 1088, EN 314-2 Class 3 or another named requirement must be explicitly documented in the contract and supported by the agreed certificate package.
